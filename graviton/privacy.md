# Privacy policy

**Last updated:** 17 August 2026  
**App:** Graviton (iOS)

## The short version

I do not collect anything about you. The app has no account, no analytics and no crash reporting, and it makes no network request of its own. It shows one advertisement, and that advertisement is served by Google, which does collect data. Inside the EU and the UK you are asked once how that advertisement may be personalised, and you can change your answer at any time.

In the words of the GDPR, I am the *controller* — the person who decides what happens with data. Google is responsible for its own handling of what its advertising collects, which is described further down.

## What the app reads from your device

The app reads your device's **motion sensor** to work out which way gravity pulls. That is how a spirit level works, and it is the whole of what the app reads.

These readings are used on your device, in the moment, to draw the instrument. They are never stored, never sent anywhere and never seen by anyone but you. iOS does not require your permission for this sensor, so the app does not ask for any — and it asks for no other permission either. There is no tracking prompt, because the app does not track you.

The app does **not** use your location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos, calendar, health data or files.

## What the app stores on your device

Three things, kept on your device only:

- **Calibration** — the correction that makes your phone read true, per Posture.
- **Language** — whether you chose English, Deutsch, or to follow the device.
- **Whether you have seen the introduction.**

There is no account, no cloud sync and no backup of these by the app. Google's consent tool separately records the answer you gave it, in the way Google's own tools do.

## Advertising

The app is free and shows one advertisement, at the top of the measuring screen. It is served by **Google AdMob**.

Google, not this app, decides what the advertisement is and what data it uses to decide. Google may collect information such as a device advertising identifier, approximate location derived from an IP address, and information about interactions with the advertisement. Google's own handling of that data is described here:

- Google's Privacy Policy: <https://policies.google.com/privacy>
- How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: <https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>

I do not receive, see or store any of that information. The app sends Google nothing about you beyond what the advertising SDK collects on its own.

**No other third party receives anything.** There is no analytics provider, no crash reporter, no attribution network and no advertising mediation.

### Your choice

**If you are in the EU or the UK**, Google asks you once — through its own consent form, on the way out of the introduction — how advertising may be personalised. Your answer is recorded by Google's consent tool, and it is what makes personalised advertising lawful. You can change it at any time: **Settings → Privacy → Ad privacy choices**.

**Elsewhere**, Google does not ask, because it is not required to. That row is then not shown in Settings, because there would be no form behind it.

Either way, if no answer allows an advertisement, the app simply shows none.

If you refuse personalised advertising, the app still works exactly as before. Nothing in the instrument is withheld: full accuracy, all three Postures, Calibration and Tolerance are free and always have been.

## Where the data goes

Google is an American company, so what its advertising collects leaves the EU and is handled in the United States and in other countries where Google operates. Google states that it relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and on standard contractual clauses for those transfers. Google's own terms describe this.

Nothing else leaves your device. The app has no server of its own to send anything to.

## How long things are kept

**I keep nothing, so there is nothing for me to keep for a length of time.**

- **On your device:** Calibration, Language and whether you have seen the introduction stay until you delete the app. Deleting the app removes them.
- **Google:** how long Google keeps what its advertising collects is set by Google, and is described in Google's own policy linked above.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you may ask to see the data held about you, to have it corrected, to have it deleted, to have its use restricted, to object to its use, to have it handed to you in a portable form, and to withdraw a consent you gave.

Write to app.graviton@gmail.com and I will answer within one month.

Two honest notes about what those answers will be:

- **What I hold about you is nothing**, so a request to see, correct or delete it has nothing to act on. That is not a refusal — it is the whole answer.
- **What Google holds is Google's**, and I cannot reach it. Use **Settings → Privacy → Ad privacy choices** to withdraw your consent, and Google's own privacy tools for the rest.

You may also complain to a data protection authority. Mine is the Bavarian one:
